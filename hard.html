Souffle recipe to bake

How to bake souffle

1. • Preheat your oven to 325°F (160°C) (or 375°F–400°F depending on specific crust preference; 325°F–375°F works best for an even bake). • Generously butter a 6-cup soufflé dish (or individual ramekins), brushing upward along the sides to encourage a clean rise. Dust the inside with grated Parmesan. 2. Make the base (Roux & Béchamel): • Melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat. Add the flour, salt, pepper, and nutmeg, whisking constantly for 2 minutes to cook out the raw flour taste. • Gradually whisk in the warm milk. Cook over medium heat, stirring continuously, until the mixture thickens smoothly and comes to a gentle simmer. • Remove the pan from the heat and stir in the shredded Cheddar/Gruyère until completely melted and smooth. Let it cool slightly, then whisk in the 4 egg yolks one by one. 3. Whip the egg whites: • In a scrupulously clean, grease-free glass or metal bowl, beat the 4 egg whites with a pinch of salt and cream of tartar (if using) using an electric mixer until stiff, glossy peaks form. (Be careful not to overbeat into a dry, clumpy texture). 4. Fold together: • Stir about one-third of the whipped egg whites into the warm cheese-yolk base to lighten the consistency. • Gently fold in the remaining egg whites in two batches using a spatula, moving in a J-shape motion to keep as much trapped air as possible. 5. Bake: • Pour the batter into your prepared soufflé dish (fill to about ¾ full or up to the inner ridge). Run your thumb or a small spatula around the inside rim to create a small "hat" groove so it rises evenly. • Bake for 35 to 55 minutes (depending on your dish size and oven) until puffed, golden brown, and set in the center. • Crucial Rule: Do not open the oven door while it is baking, or the sudden temperature drop will cause it to deflate. 6. Serve immediately: • Soufflés start dropping within minutes of coming out of the heat, so bring it straight to the table and serve at once.
